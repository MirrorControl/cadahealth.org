ABA Access Project

A health equity initiative addressing the systems failures that deny Black and Brown families equal access to autism behavioral health services.

"Every child with autism deserves the full benefit of the services they were authorized to receive. Right now, the families with the least power are the most likely to lose them. We are here to change that."
The Problem

An authorization crisis hiding inside a care crisis

Applied Behavior Analysis is the gold standard treatment for autism spectrum disorder, endorsed by the American Academy of Pediatrics and covered by Medicaid and most commercial insurers under the Mental Health Parity Act. And yet the gap between coverage and actual delivered care is enormous — and growing. The families with the greatest need are navigating a system designed, in practice, to exhaust them.

Who Is Being Left Behind

Black and Brown families face compounded barriers

The ABA access crisis is not distributed evenly. Black and Hispanic children are diagnosed later, receive fewer authorized hours, and encounter more institutional barriers than white children at every stage of the process. This is not a gap in availability — it is a gap in power.

22% of Black parents reported experiencing direct racism from professionals during the autism diagnosis and treatment process — having their concerns dismissed, being pushed toward misdiagnosis, or being told their child did not qualify for services they were entitled to.

Vanderbilt University and Meharry Medical College study, cited in SPARK for Autism research, 2024

"There are zero ABA providers in communities of color." This is not an exaggeration. It reflects the lived reality reported directly by Black and Brown parents in research on autism service disparities.

Parent testimony, SPARK for Autism community research, 2024

ProPublica documented that UnitedHealth Group's mental health division Optum was using internal cost-cutting protocols to deliberately limit ABA access for Medicaid enrolled children — strategies that could affect up to 1 in 5 children currently receiving ABA therapy and disproportionately impact Medi-Cal families.

ProPublica investigative report, 2025

Children's behavioral outcomes measurably worsen the longer they wait on ABA waitlists. Research published in the Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis in 2025 documented the direct relationship between authorization gaps and behavioral deterioration. These are not abstract statistics. These are children whose development regressed because a paperwork system failed them.

The Core Mechanism

The documentation gap no one is teaching around

ABA authorizations are renewed based on clinical documentation submitted by therapists and reviewed by payers. The quality and precision of that documentation directly determines whether a child keeps their authorized hours at renewal. This is not widely understood at the frontline therapist level — where most of the documentation is actually written.

Frontline Registered Behavior Technicians complete the majority of session documentation in ABA. They are trained rigorously on therapy technique and behavioral intervention. They receive almost no training on how their notes function inside the payer authorization system — what language demonstrates medical necessity, what documentation supports renewal, and what documentation inadvertently triggers a denial.

The families most likely to lose services due to documentation failures are the families at Medi-Cal only clinics — smaller, under-resourced providers serving the lowest income and most marginalized communities. Large corporate ABA providers have full compliance teams built around this knowledge. Small Medi-Cal clinics often do not. This is an information equity problem with direct consequences for real children.

The Initiative

Two tracks. One system of accountability.

The ABA Access Project addresses authorization equity through two interconnected tracks. The therapist-facing track builds documentation competency inside the clinical system. The family-facing track builds advocacy capacity outside it. Together, they create a more accountable pipeline at every point where authorizations can be lost.

Track 1

ABA Authorization Documentation Certificate

A professional certification for frontline ABA therapists covering authorization documentation standards, session note quality, and the downstream payer impact of clinical records. Free for therapists at Medi-Cal only clinics, funded through grant support. Market rate for all others.

  • Five module curriculum covering the full authorization lifecycle, documentation standards, and payer communication
  • Direct focus on CPT codes 97151 through 97158 and medical necessity language across Medi-Cal and commercial payer frameworks
  • Ethics module establishing the therapist's fiduciary obligation to their client, not the payer
  • Certificate valid for two years, renewable — a professional credential therapists carry on their profiles
  • Free access funded by health equity grants for therapists at Medi-Cal serving clinics
  • Creates a parent-readable signal: families can ask "Does your therapist hold the ABA authorization documentation certification?"
Track 2

ABA Insurance Navigation Resources for Families

Free, plain language guides and tools for families navigating ABA authorization, benefits verification, denial appeals, and payer transitions. Specifically centered on Black and Brown families and Medi-Cal populations who face the highest barriers and have the fewest existing resources.

  • ABA Insurance Navigation Guide: what families are entitled to, how to check authorization status, and how to appeal a denial
  • ABA Authorization Appeal Toolkit: fillable appeal letter templates citing Mental Health Parity Act rights and clinical necessity standards
  • Red flags resource: what to do when something feels wrong, including the right to request all clinical records
  • Payer transition guide: how to protect service continuity when insurance changes
  • Distributed through Autism Society chapters, FEAT, disability rights organizations, and community health organizations

Built-in Ethics Framework

Any knowledge system can be misused. The certification explicitly addresses this. The ethics module teaches that documentation written to sabotage a client's authorization is a clinical ethics violation, a potential HIPAA violation, and in some cases insurance fraud. Certificate holders sign a code of ethics as a condition of issuance — creating a documented professional standard and an accountability mechanism when families file complaints. The family navigation resources include explicit guidance on requesting clinical records, filing board complaints, and accessing Medi-Cal fair hearings. The two tracks work together: the certification creates accountability at the therapist level; the family resources create oversight from the outside.
